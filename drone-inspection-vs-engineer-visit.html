Drone Inspection vs. an Engineer Visit: Which Is Better for Your Solar Roof?

July 7, 2026 6 min read Sunlytics team

When you decide to get serious about solar energy, one of the first steps is a roof assessment. Most people automatically call a few installers and wait for an engineer to show up. But in 2026, there's another path that saves time, saves money, and delivers more accurate data.

In this article, we'll give you an honest comparison of both methods so you can decide what fits your situation best.

How a traditional engineering site visit works

The traditional process looks like this: you call an installer or an independent engineer, agree on a visit date (usually a 1-2 week wait), the specialist arrives at your property, manually measures the roof, visually assesses shading, and delivers a quote a few days later.

This system has worked for decades and has real advantages: an engineer can spot structural issues, talk with you on-site, and answer questions. But it also has significant drawbacks.

Problems with a traditional site visit

  • Wait time: 1-3 weeks before the visit
  • Price: €200-600 for an independent engineering assessment
  • Measurement accuracy: manual measurement carries a ±3-5% margin of error
  • Shading analysis: often done visually, without precise calculations
  • Each installer delivers a different quote using a different methodology, making them hard to compare

How a drone roof inspection works

A drone inspection works completely differently. A verified pilot (holding the drone operator credentials required in their country) arrives at your property and flies over the roof at 35 meters for 20-30 minutes, capturing 250-350 high-resolution photos with 80% overlap. The photos are fed into an analysis system, which, within a few hours, produces:

  • A precise roof topography map with a ±10 cm margin of error
  • A 3D digital surface model (DSM) with the height of every obstruction
  • A 365-day, hour-by-hour shading analysis
  • Recommended system size calculations
  • An annual energy production forecast based on PVGIS data
  • A 25-year financial return analysis

All of it is delivered in a professional PDF report within 24 hours.

When to choose a drone, when to choose an engineer

Choose a drone inspection when:

  • You want a fast, accurate initial data assessment
  • You're planning to get quotes from several installers and want an independent baseline to compare them against
  • You have a commercial property with a complex roof and many obstructions
  • You want a professional report you can present to a bank or a partner
  • You just want to quickly find out whether it's worth investing more time into solar

Choose a traditional site visit when:

  • You've already picked an installer and want a final quote
  • There are specific structural questions that need a physical specialist
  • The roof has potential structural issues that need to be assessed on-site

The smartest approach: many customers use both methods. They start by ordering a drone report (€149), get precise data, and then approach installers with concrete numbers already in hand. This lets them compare quotes more fairly and negotiate from a stronger position. The drone report pays for itself with the first percentage point saved in negotiations.

Does a drone report replace an engineer?

Not entirely, and it's important to understand this correctly. A drone report gives you highly accurate architectural data and solar potential analysis, but it doesn't remove the need for a certified electrician or structural engineer during the actual installation phase.

Think of it this way: a drone report is a digital X-ray you get before seeing the doctor. It doesn't replace the doctor, but it lets them work far more efficiently because they know exactly where to look. Installers who receive customers with drone reports already in hand report that preliminary planning work drops by 70-80%.

What is a verified drone pilot, and why does it matter

As an EU member state, Finland requires that commercial drone flights over private property be carried out only by remote pilots registered with Traficom (the Finnish Transport and Communications Agency), the country's national aviation authority. This ensures:

  • Safe flight in compliance with airspace regulations
  • Drone insurance during the flight
  • Clear liability in case of damage
  • Data quality that meets professional standards

Every pilot on a Sunlytics mission is verified and registered with Traficom or their home country's equivalent aviation authority.
