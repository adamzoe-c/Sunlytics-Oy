How Much Does a Solar System Cost in Finland in 2026, and Is It Worth Investing?

July 6, 2026 8 min read Sunlytics team

In 2026, the solar market in Finland is more mature than ever. Equipment prices have dropped sharply over the past decade, and Finland's long summer days mean systems produce more per installed kilowatt than many people expect from a Nordic country. Still, many people don't know where to start, or what the real price and payback actually look like once you factor in Finland's electricity prices and the fact that Finland doesn't have a retail net-metering scheme for households, as opposed to a marketing brochure figure.

This article is for anyone in the Helsinki or Tampere area seriously considering a solar system who wants to understand the numbers before talking to installers.

Key factors that determine a solar system's price

Before getting into specific numbers, it's worth understanding what actually drives the price. In our experience, two properties with similar roofs can end up with very different prices for a few reasons:

  • System power (kWp): the larger the system, the lower the price per kilowatt.
  • Roof type: flat roofs are usually more expensive to install on than pitched roofs, due to the need for special mounting structures.
  • Roof orientation and shading: a south-facing roof with no shade is ideal. East- or west-facing roofs reduce efficiency by 15-25%.
  • Distance and infrastructure: the further the inverter is from the electrical panel, the more cabling and labor cost.
  • Inverter type: string inverters are cheaper, but modular options (microinverters or optimizers) work better on shaded roofs.

Methodology note: annual output assumes roughly 800-950 kWh per installed kWp per year, based on typical PVGIS-style irradiation estimates for the Helsinki/Tampere area. Savings are calculated using a blended electricity value that combines self-consumed power (around €0.20/kWh, close to Finland's average household retail price including taxes and grid fees) with exported surplus, which Finland pays back at a spot-market-linked price that is usually far lower (~€0.04-0.08/kWh), since Finland has no retail net-metering scheme for households — a system only gets credited for what it uses within the same short settlement window, and everything beyond that is sold, not credited. Systems sized for higher self-consumption pay back faster; systems that mostly export surplus pay back more slowly.

Will a solar system pay off in Finland?

Short answer: yes, but payback usually takes longer than in Central or Southern European countries with a working net-metering scheme. Finland's average household electricity price sits below the EU average, and exported surplus isn't credited at the retail rate — but Finland's long summer daylight hours partly make up for the northern latitude. The economics work best when you size a system for your own consumption, or add an EV or heat pump that can soak up summer daytime production, rather than aiming for maximum output.

Let's compare solar with other investments:

  • Bank deposit in 2026: 2-3% annual return
  • Rental apartment in Helsinki: roughly 4-5% gross annual yield
  • Solar system: 6-11% annual return for a well-sized home system, higher for commercial installations (depending on self-consumption and the property)

A solar system also increases property value. Studies across Europe show that homes with solar panels sell for 4-6% more on average.

Commercial properties: why it's an even better investment

In a residential home, most of the electricity generated by a solar system gets used in the evening, at night, or in winter, when the system isn't producing. Since Finland doesn't have retail net metering, that surplus gets sold at a low, spot-linked price instead of being credited at the retail price — which makes self-consumption timing matter even more here than in countries with net metering.

In a commercial building, it's the opposite: electricity gets used during the day, exactly when the system produces the most, especially through Finland's long summer daylight hours. That means almost all the generated energy is consumed on-site immediately at the full retail rate, and payback can be as short as 6-8 years.

Real-world example: a warehouse or production facility with a 50 kWp system, operating weekdays 8am-6pm, typically pays back in 6-7 years and generates roughly €150,000-€210,000 in net profit over 25 years, depending on consumption.

Common mistakes when choosing a solar system

  • Choosing based on price alone. The cheapest system often uses lower-quality panels that produce 15-20% less electricity after 10 years. Think in terms of a 25-year outlook.
  • Skipping a roof analysis before buying. Shade from a chimney or a neighboring house can cut system efficiency by 20-40%. It's important to know this before investing.
  • Forgetting about maintenance. Solar panels need minimal upkeep, but heavy snow accumulation should be cleared occasionally and inverters can occasionally fail and need repair. Negotiate the warranty terms.
  • Not registering the grid connection early. Your local distribution network operator (DSO) needs to approve and register the connection before you can export surplus. Start this process as early as possible, especially before the busy spring installation season.
